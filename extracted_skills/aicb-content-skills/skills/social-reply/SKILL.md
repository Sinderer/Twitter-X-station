# Social reply

## What it is

An answer under someone else's post: a reply on X and Threads, a comment on
Reddit and Instagram. The target is the post answered (its URL, author and
text); the text is the reply. The result is the reply's own URL.

Replies are the cheapest content and the most visible character of an account:
where it sounds most like a person or most like a bot, where it earns the
replies to its own posts, and where it gets banned. Most replies come from a
mention or keyword watch you run (any tool, or searching by hand); some from
the user's own link; and the ones that matter most are under the account's own
posts.

## Where the target comes from

**A watch you run** (a mention or keyword watch, with any tool, or searching by
hand). What kind of watch found the post says what you are looking at and what
the reply is for:

| kind | what it finds | the reply |
|---|---|---|
| own posts | replies under the account's own posts | the user's conversation: answer the real questions in the first hour, thank the specific praise, let the drive-bys be; this is community management, and it decides whether the next post gets replies |
| mentions | posts naming the account | decide on each: a question gets an answer, a complaint gets an answer and a way to continue in DM, praise gets a short human thanks, a troll gets nothing |
| keyword | posts on the account's topic | answer only when the account has something to add (the number, the case, the fix); never to be present |
| author | posts by people the user follows | the account is a regular under them: short, in character, not every post, never the first reply every time |
| community | a subreddit or a circle | the account is a member there, with the member's manners and the sub's rules; answer OP's actual question |

The user's own instruction for a watch outranks this table (they wrote what
they want done with these finds). The triage: a batch of twenty finds becomes
three to five replies, the rest skipped with the reason in the note.

**The user's link** («reply to this one», «write under this that…»).

**Nothing else.** A reply is never written to a post you have not read in
full, with the thread above it and the replies already under it: open it in
the browser, or with whatever social-data tool is connected (on Reddit, the
post URL with `.json` appended returns the post with its comments). A reply to
the headline of a post that says the opposite in paragraph two, or a point made
thirty times in the replies already, is the classic self-inflicted ratio.

## Before writing

- the voice notes in `NOTES.md` (languages, style, rules, examples — how this
  account talks to people, not how it posts), whether the account has X
  Premium (280 characters without Premium), the posting hours (replies have
  hours too: an account that answers at 4 a.m. every night is a bot).
- the user's own instructions for replies, if they wrote some down in
  `NOTES.md` — they override this skill.
- the `reply` notes in `NOTES.md` for standing rules ("don't argue", "in r/…
  don't mention the product", "don't answer hate", "a link to the episode is
  fine").
- the last thirty replies in the working folder — what the account has said
  this week and to whom; the same account replies to the same post once (a
  second message in a real conversation is the one deliberate exception).

## What a good reply is

- **It answers that post**, in its own words, at the point where the post is.
  A reply that could sit under any post sits under none. Quote a phrase of the
  post when it helps («about the "free" part — there is one payment they don't
  show on the landing page»).
- **It adds one thing**: the answer, the counter-example, the number, the
  source, the fix, the question that moves the conversation, the thing the
  author did not know. It does not restate the post and agree with it; it does
  not summarise the thread.
- **Short.** One to three sentences on X and Threads; on Reddit as long as the
  answer needs, in paragraphs, and no longer. The first sentence is the reply;
  the rest is evidence. A reply longer than the post it answers is a post.
- **In the account's voice**, to a person. «Great question!» is a bot; «yes,
  and the catch is X» is a person. The language is the post's when the account
  speaks it (the languages in the voice notes), the account's otherwise.
- **The product is a perspective, never the subject.** A reply that names the
  product is a reply the user asked for, or an answer to a direct question
  («what do you use?»), and even then it is one line with no link unless asked.
- **Never the same twice.** Identical or near-identical replies across posts
  are spam to the platform and to the readers; vary the angle, or skip. One
  reply per post per account; two of the user's accounts under one post is
  the one thing that gets all of them banned on Reddit and throttled on X.

**Replies that work**, with their shapes:

| shape | example |
|---|---|
| the answer with the number | «It's 12 %, not 40 — the 40 is the 2019 survey. The method changed in 2023.» |
| the case | «We hit this in March. The fix was the vendor's 30 s default; nobody had read the config.» |
| the better question | «Which part is slow for you — the first build or every build? They have different fixes.» |
| the fix | «`--preset veryfast` and it drops to 2 min. The slow part is the subtitle burn, not the cut.» |
| the specific thanks (under praise) | «The third chart took the longest and I almost cut it — glad it was the one.» |
| the disagreement with a reason | «I'd push back on one thing: the queue is the bottleneck after this, not the model. Ours was at 400 by week two.» |
| the honest «don't know» (under own posts) | «No idea yet — we only have four weeks of data. Will post when it's twelve.» |

**Replies that fail:** «Great post!», «This 👆», «So true», a reply that is only
a link, a reply that corrects a typo, a reply that argues with a stranger's
opinion about taste, a reply that starts with «Actually» or «Well,», a reply
under a post older than a day that nobody is reading any more (this is why a
watch filters for freshness), a reply in the wrong language, a reply to a
pile-on (the account becomes part of it), a reply to a troll (they wanted the
reply), a reply with an @ of someone not in the conversation, a reply that is
the same joke the account made yesterday.

## Per platform

- **X:** the inline box under the post; 280 characters without Premium. What X
  ranks under a post: the author's own replies, then replies from accounts the
  author follows or has interacted with, then the rest by engagement, with
  Premium replies boosted — so the first hour and a reply the author answers
  are worth more than ten replies elsewhere. A reply to a reply goes under the
  reply, not under the root. A link the user gave stays verbatim; X shows a
  reply with a link to fewer people, and that trade is accepted (it is also the
  normal place for the account's own link under its own post). Media is allowed
  and rarely needed; a screenshot that answers the question is the exception.
- **Threads:** the same, 500 characters; a reply in a thread you did not start
  is shown to the author's followers first; Threads surfaces replies in the feed
  on their own, so a good reply is a post.
- **Reddit:** a comment in the sub's register. Read the sub's rules first
  (`https://www.reddit.com/r/<sub>/about/rules.json` in the browser, or
  whatever social-data tool is connected): no links, no self-promo, flair,
  minimum karma or account age, «no low-effort». Link-free unless the brief
  demands one; subs remove self-promo links and the account with them. No
  markdown headers, no bullet lists unless the sub uses them; a question back
  to OP is a normal close; «OP» means the post's author. Sort by new before
  replying: a comment under a thread with 400 comments is read by nobody,
  while the fourth comment under a fresh post is read by everyone who opens it.
  Downvoted comments are not deleted (deleting reads worse than the downvotes);
  the next one is written better. One account, one opinion per thread: never
  two of the user's accounts in one comment tree, never an upvote from a
  second account.
- **Instagram:** a comment under a post; short, no links, no hashtags; a
  question gets an answer, praise gets a word; comments on other accounts'
  posts are read by their audience, so they are the account's voice in public.

## Volume and pacing

A reply is cheap to write and expensive to be seen doing in bulk. The posting
hours and how often the watch runs set the rhythm: a handful of replies spread
over the day, not twenty in a minute; under the account's own post, every real
question in the first hour and then the rest as they come; under other
people's posts, a few a day, never the same author three times in a row. A
reply that would be the account's tenth of the hour is skipped. The user may
set a cap in `NOTES.md` ("no more than five a day"); the default is restraint.

## The user's own posts

The replies under the account's posts are the user's conversation, and the
account's answers decide whether the next post gets replies at all:

- answer every real question in the first hour (X ranks the author's replies
  on top, and the reply pushes the post back into feeds);
- thank specific praise specifically; a 👍 or a like is enough for «great post»;
- a correction that is right gets «you're right, fixed» and the fix; a
  correction that is wrong gets the source, once;
- a complaint gets an answer and, when it is a customer, a way to continue
  («DM me, we'll sort it out»), never a defence in public;
- a troll gets nothing, a pile-on gets nothing; the user decides whether to
  mute or block;
- the account's own link (the episode, the article, the product) goes as the
  first reply under its own post when the user wants it there.

## Handing over

Write the reply into a dated `.md` in the working folder, with the target above
it — the post's URL, its author and its text — so the user sees what is being
answered without opening it. For a Reddit comment the post's URL is the
target; for a reply to a reply, the reply's URL. Show it to the user (a whole
batch at once when the finds came in a batch); nothing is posted until they
say so. After it is live, note the reply's own URL in the file and mark the
find as handled in your watch list.

## After

- An answer to the reply is a new find under own posts or mentions; the
  conversation is one reply at a time, and it ends when the account has
  nothing to add (the last word is not a goal).
- Note under `reply:learned` in `NOTES.md`: which threads were worth it, which
  subs want what, whom the account should stop answering, what the user
  changed in review, which replies the authors answered.
- A reply that got more likes than the post it answered is a post next week.

## Checklist before the user sees it

- the post read in full, with the thread and the replies under it; nothing
  already said thirty times
- answers that post; adds one thing; first sentence is the reply
- one to three sentences (X / Threads), paragraphs only on Reddit; 280 / 500
- in the account's voice, to a person; no «Great post!», no «Actually»
- product only if asked; no link unless the user's and allowed on the platform
- Reddit: sub rules read, no self-promo, sorted by new, one account per thread
- not a duplicate (same post, same account) and not the same reply as elsewhere
- inside posting hours, inside the day's restraint; the target noted in full (URL, author, text); the find marked handled after
