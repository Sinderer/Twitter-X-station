# Social thread

## What it is

A chain of connected posts on X or Threads, read top to bottom: a story, a
build log, a breakdown, a list told in parts, a set of findings from one
dataset. The text holds the parts as paragraphs separated by **one blank
line**; on X they are posted in order from one composer (`/compose/post`, the
＋ button per part, one Post for all); on Threads the parts are a chain of
replies under the first post. The thread's URL is the first part's.

## When a thread, and when not

- A **post** holds one idea. The moment the second idea needs its own line of
  thought, it is a thread.
- A thread is **three to eight parts**. Fewer is a post with line breaks; more
  is an X Article (`x-article`, X Premium+) or two threads a week apart. The
  samples that did well on both platforms sit at five to seven.
- A thread is for things that **unfold**: what happened and what it cost, how
  something was built, the five mistakes behind one result, a list where every
  item needs a sentence of why, one dataset and its findings. A hot take is a
  post; an opinion with evidence is a thread; a story with a turn is a thread.
- A thread is **not** a long post cut into 280-character pieces: each part is a
  post that could stand in the feed on its own, and the cut points are ideas,
  not character counts.

## Before writing, read what is already there

- the voice notes in `NOTES.md` (languages, style, rules, examples — look
  specifically for how the account has done threads before: numbered or not,
  media or not, how it closes), whether the account has X Premium. **Non-Premium
  X: 280 characters per part, every part**, four images or one video per part.
  Premium: long parts are allowed and still a bad idea; a thread of long posts
  is an article nobody asked for. **Threads: 500 characters per part**, up to
  ten images per part.
- the rubric brief, when the thread belongs to one; the `thread` notes in
  `NOTES.md` for what the user said about threads ("no numbering", "no more
  than five", "the first part always with a screenshot").
- the last ten threads in the working folder — the stories already told. A
  thread is remembered longer than a post; a retold one reads as recycling.
  Look at which first parts got the replies.
- the user's own instructions for threads, if they wrote some down in
  `NOTES.md` — they override this skill.

## The shape

**Part 1 is a post that must survive alone.** It is the only part the feed
shows, the only part most readers will ever see, and the only part that decides
whether anyone opens the rest. It carries the claim or the result, not the
setup:

> I made ants solve a maze in 48 hours. Here is what broke, in order.

> We cut our render bill from $900 to $140 a month. Three changes; the first
> one was a typo.

Not: «A thread on something I've been thinking about 🧵», «Let me explain»,
«1/». If part 1 would not be posted on its own, the thread is not ready.
Numbering («1/», «2/») is a style choice the account already made or not
(the voice examples); the «🧵» opener and «a thread:» are clichés unless the
account owns them; «(1/7)» at the end of each part is the one numbering that
never looks like a template.

**Parts 2…n-1 carry one idea each**, in the order a reader needs them, not the
order things happened. Every part has a concrete thing in it: a number, a
screenshot, a quote, a before/after, a decision and why. A part that only
connects two others («but that was not the end of it») is cut, and a part with
two ideas is split. Sentences stay short: a part is read on a phone between two
other posts, and the second sentence of a part is where readers leave. The
cuts land on the ideas; when an idea does not fit a part, the idea is trimmed,
not continued in the next part mid-sentence.

**The last part closes.** The takeaway in one line, the question to the
audience, or the one link the user gave (verbatim). «Thanks for reading» is
not a close; «follow for more» is the account's choice in the voice rules, not
the default; «RT the first post» is a request the audience grants to threads
that earned it. A thread that ends on its strongest line gets quoted by it.

## Formats that hold up

| format | skeleton |
|---|---|
| the story | what I set out to do → what happened (with the number) → what it cost → what I learned → the one line |
| the build log | the goal → step 1 with evidence → step 2 → … → the result (screenshot) → what is next |
| the list | the claim (part 1) → n items, one per part, each with a why and a number → the one that matters most |
| the teardown | someone else's thing (name it, no @) → what it does well → what it misses → what to take from it |
| the numbers | one dataset, where it comes from → finding 1 → … → the one nobody expected → what it means |
| the mistakes | the result nobody believed → mistake 1 and its cost → … → the fix → the rule now |
| the timeline | a day or a year in parts, each with a timestamp, ending on now |

Read the format off the material, not off the last thread: an account whose
every thread is a list is an account with one trick.

## Research

A thread without numbers, names, screenshots or quotes is a long post. Before
writing:

- the user's own material first: what they told you, their files in the
  working folder, their posts (their profile in the browser, or with whatever
  social-data tool is connected) — a post of theirs with sixty replies is a
  thread with its objections pre-collected;
- the platform: search X for the topic for how the topic is argued now, the
  replies under the posts that did well for the objections each part should
  answer, the trends page when the thread rides a trend; a Threads search for
  Threads;
- the facts: a Google search, Google News, the browser — or whatever search
  or social-data tool is connected.

Nothing in a part is invented: a number without a place it came from is cut or
replaced by what the user actually measured; a quote is a real one with the
person named, or it is a paraphrase without quotation marks.

## Media

The media files sit next to the text in the working folder (a screenshot, or a
picture from the `find-images` skill / an image generator you have, local or
web); they are attached in order, one or more per part — say in the part's
text which image goes there («[screenshot 2]») and remove the marker before
saving. A screenshot of the real thing beats a generated graphic; a generated
graphic beats text; a part with a picture is read twice as often as a part
without. Video: one per thread, H.264 + AAC, ≤ 2:20 on non-Premium X, usually
in part 1 or in the result part. Not every part needs a picture; a thread that
is eight screenshots with captions is a gallery.

## Language and voice

The audience's language (the languages in the voice notes), the account's
register (the style and the examples). A thread is where a person sounds most
like themselves, because it is long enough to hear a rhythm: vary sentence
length, let a part be one sentence when it earns it, let the joke land in part
4 and not in every part. The product is a perspective, not the subject: one
part out of eight may say what the user sells, and only when the thread led
there; the link to it goes in the close or in a reply, never in part 1.

## Per platform

- **X:** written in one composer, all parts, one Post; check each part's
  counter before adding the next (a part over the limit blocks the whole
  thread). A thread a day at most; the first part scheduled in the posting
  hours. Replies under part 1 are where the conversation happens; answering
  them in the first hour is the `social-reply` skill's job (watching the user's
  own posts). A link in part 1 lowers the thread's reach; the link goes in the
  close.
- **Threads:** the first post, then each part as a reply to the previous one,
  500 characters each; links are fine; a Threads thread is read as a
  conversation with yourself, so a looser, spoken register works there.

## Handing over

Write the thread into a dated `.md` in the working folder: one blank line
between parts, the numbers (if any) in the text, the media files next to it
with which part each one belongs to. Show it to the user with the character
count per part; nothing is posted until they say so. After it is live, note
the URL of part 1 in the file.

## After

Note under `thread:learned` in `NOTES.md`: which part 1 opened, which length
held, which format the audience answered, where readers dropped (the platform
shows it per part). Threads are the account's long memory; the next one should
know what the last one did. A thread that did well is an X Article
(`x-article`) next month, with the replies as its FAQ.

## Checklist before the user sees it

- part 1 stands alone and carries the claim or the result; no «🧵», no «let me explain»
- three to eight parts; one idea per part; every part has a concrete thing
- each part inside the limit (280 non-Premium X, 500 Threads); cuts on ideas
- the last part closes on the strongest line, the question or the user's link
- numbers and quotes real; the user's own material first
- media attached in order, markers removed from the text; video H.264
- in the audience's language, in the account's register; product at most one part, not part 1
- not a story already told (the threads in the working folder); one blank line between parts
