# Social post

## What it is

One post on the account: text, up to four images or one video. The shortest
piece there is and the one written most often, so it is where the account's
voice is heard or lost. On X and Threads it is the unit of the feed; on Reddit
it is a submission with a title and a body; on Instagram a text-only post does
not exist (that is an image post: a picture with a caption).

The post is the text plus the files; the user posts it from their own account.
This skill is what the post is and what decides whether it is good.

## Before writing, read what is already there

Everything a post needs is in the project's `NOTES.md` and the working folder.
Read it rather than ask:

- the voice notes in `NOTES.md` →
  - the languages: the language, and it is the audience's, not the chat's;
  - the style and the rules: the register, what never appears (competitors,
    prices, politics, clients by name, emoji, hashtags);
  - the examples: the posts that sound like them. Examples beat adjectives
    every time — "write it lively and to the point" describes nothing, three
    of their own posts that worked describe everything;
  - the bio: what the account is for, which is what the product is to the
    posts: a perspective, not a subject;
  - whether the account has X Premium (X without Premium: 280 characters, the
    composer refuses the rest), the timezone, the posting hours.
- **the rubric**, when the post belongs to a recurring column: its topic and
  brief say what this rubric is about and what one post in it looks like. A
  post for a rubric slot fills that slot; do not write a second one next to it.
- the `post` notes in `NOTES.md` and the notes a rubric keeps: what worked,
  what to avoid, decisions ("nobody reads past three paragraphs", "we don't
  post on Mondays", "the link always goes in the reply").
- the last twenty posts in the working folder: do not repeat an angle, an
  opening or a joke; keep the continuity of voice; see what got replies.
- what is already drafted and waiting in the folder, so two posts do not say
  the same thing on the same day.
- the user's own instructions for posts, if they wrote some down in `NOTES.md`
  — they override everything below.

Priority when they conflict, most specific wins: the user's own `post`
instructions > the rubric brief > the voice rules > the notes > this skill.

## When the user has not said enough

Ask for the few things you cannot invent, in their words, and stop when you
have enough to write one post they would recognise:

- **what the account is for and who reads it** — one sentence each;
- **accounts they like**, two or three, and *what exactly* they like there (the
  hooks, the length, the humour, that every post is a story). Read them (their
  profile in the browser, or with whatever social-data tool is connected) and
  take the pattern, not the words;
- **their own posts that worked** — the strongest signal there is, because it
  already sounds like them and already landed;
- **anything they hated** from a previous attempt, theirs or someone else's. A
  rejected example teaches faster than three approved ones;
- **what must never appear.** Cheaper to hear once than to discover in review.

If they have nothing to show, write two or three posts in visibly different
registers and let them point. Choosing is easy; describing is not. Then write
what you learned where it belongs: the pattern into the voice examples / voice
rules in `NOTES.md`, the rubric's decisions into the rubric brief, and when the
user has their own way of doing posts ("Spanish only, no hashtags, always a
question at the end"), into their own `post` instructions in `NOTES.md`.

## What a good post is

- **Hook first.** The first line earns the stop: a sharp claim, a number, a
  tension, a contrarian take, a concrete image, a question the reader has. No
  warm-up, no «in today's world», no «I've been thinking lately». On X and
  Threads the first line is the whole post for most readers; on Reddit the
  title is.
- **Concrete.** At least one tangible thing: a real scenario, a number, a named
  situation, a date, «we tried X and Y happened». A platitude with no example
  behind it («the real work is building systems») is not a post; the example
  is.
- **A point of view.** Something only this account would say, that someone
  could disagree with. Generic thought-leadership phrasing is the failure mode
  of the genre; agreement with everyone is invisible.
- **Human.** Written like a sharp person posting, not an account. Sentence
  length varies; blunt and funny are fine; a typo is fine, a hashtag wall is
  not. No «🧵», no «Let that sink in», no «Thread below», no «Agree?».
- **One idea.** A second idea is a second post or a thread.
- **The audience's language** (the languages in the voice notes). People brief
  in one language and publish in another all the time; the language of the
  chat means nothing. Not set and nobody said: ask once, write it into
  `NOTES.md`.
- **The product is a perspective, not the subject.** At most one post in five
  carries what the user sells, and it carries it as a fact in a story, not as
  a pitch. On Reddit: none.

**Hooks that work** (pick the one the material supports, not the strongest one):

| hook | example shape |
|---|---|
| the number | «48 hours. The ants solved the maze.» / «$0.30 against $40.» |
| the claim | «Most ‹X› advice is written by people who never did ‹X›.» |
| the tension | «We shipped it in a week. Then we deleted it in a day.» |
| the scene | «3 a.m., the render at 40 %, and the fan starts clicking.» |
| the contrarian | «Stop measuring ‹Y›. It never predicted anything we cared about.» |
| the question the reader has | «Why does every ant-colony demo run at the wrong speed?» |
| the confession | «I was wrong about ‹Z› for two years. Here is the number that changed it.» |
| the list promise | «Three things that broke, in the order they broke.» (then the three) |

**Forms** to vary across a week: the hot take; the observation («noticed that…»);
the question to the audience; the mini-list (three items, each one line); the
one-liner; the story in four lines (setup, turn, result, the point); the
before/after; the how-I («how I ‹did X› in ‹time›, with the number»); the
teardown of someone's thing (without the @, unless it is praise); the quote of
their own earlier post with what changed. A week of posts in one form is a bot
account.

## Per platform

**X.**
- Non-Premium: 280 characters, the composer refuses the rest; a draft over the
  limit is cancelled and re-split (a thread), not trimmed in the composer.
  Premium: long text is still a post (`note_tweet`); even then the first 280
  are what the feed shows, so the hook is in them.
- What X ranks: replies and quotes above reposts above likes; a post that starts
  a conversation outlives a post that gets liked. A post whose only content is
  a link is shown to fewer people — the usual move is the text as the post and
  the link in the first reply (the `social-reply` skill), unless the user said
  otherwise.
- Four images or one video (H.264 + AAC, ≤ 2:20 non-Premium) per post; alt text
  on images where the composer allows it; a GIF counts as the video. One image
  with the number in it beats four screenshots.
- Hashtags: none, unless the account already uses them (the voice examples). An
  @mention of a big account in a post is a reply to them in disguise.
- Posting hours: the user's posting hours in their timezone; a post outside
  them is scheduled for the next slot, not posted at once. Two posts at least
  two hours apart; a thread counts as one.

**Threads.**
- 500 characters, up to ten images or one video, links are fine and shown.
- Threads is text-first and conversation-first: a question, an opinion, a
  short story get replies; a repost of the X post word for word reads as
  cross-posting (it is) — rewrite the opening, drop the X-isms.
- Tags: one topic tag per post is the convention, no hashtag strings.

**Reddit** — a submission, and the failure modes are a marketing headline and
self-promotion; both are downvoted or removed, and the account with them.
- **The subreddit decides** (`https://www.reddit.com/r/<sub>/about/rules.json`
  in the browser, or whatever social-data tool is connected → rules; the sub's
  page for subscribers and type): flair required, no links, no self-promo,
  minimum account age or karma, text-only or image-only, weekly threads for the
  thing you want to post. A post that breaks a rule is dropped with the reason,
  not pushed through.
- **Title** ≤ 300 characters, and the way a real person types: plain, specific,
  lowercase-ish («anyone else seeing X?», «tried X for a month, here's what
  broke», «how do you handle Y when Z?»). No colon-subtitle, no «Here's how»,
  no «The futile game of…», no clickbait, no emoji.
- **Body**: markdown, first person, two to five short paragraphs; a real
  experience, observation or question; fine to be unsure, fine to ask. End with
  a genuine question to the sub (what do you use? am I wrong? anyone solved
  this?), not a conclusion and never a CTA. No emoji bullets, no TL;DR, no
  bold-headed sections, no listicle — that reads as AI / marketing. One image
  per post through the form; galleries and video are a different tab.
- **Zero self-promotion.** No brand name, no «we built», no product pitch. The
  account's work may shape the perspective and never be the subject; when the
  post cannot be written without the brand, write about the underlying problem.
  A link to the user's own thing goes up as theirs, disclosed, in a sub that
  allows it, and rarely.
- Read the sub before the first post there (`/r/<sub>/top` in the browser, or
  `https://www.reddit.com/r/<sub>/top.json`): the titles that worked, the
  length that is normal there, what gets removed. Sort by new is where a fresh
  post competes.
- One account, one opinion per post: two of the user's accounts on one thread
  is vote manipulation and kills both.

**Instagram.** A text-only post does not exist; a picture with a caption is an
image post (caption ≤ 2,200 characters, the first line is what shows, three to
five hashtags are normal there).

## Links and sources

- A link the user gave stays character for character; never paraphrased,
  shortened or dropped. Where it goes (the post, the first reply) is the
  platform rule above unless the user said.
- Original posts carry no other links. Nothing invented: no «source:», no «by
  @artist», no made-up citation, no number without a place it came from. When a
  lookup returned nothing, say so and go on; the gap is not filled.
- A fact in a post was checked this session (a Google search, Google News, the
  browser) or it is the user's own, or it is framed as theirs («by our
  numbers»).

## Media with a post

A screenshot of the real thing beats a generated graphic; a generated graphic
beats text alone when the post is a number, a comparison or a scene (the
`find-images` skill finds; an image generator you have, local or web, makes).
The files sit next to the post's text in the working folder. Four images max
on X, ten on Threads, one on Reddit; video H.264 + AAC + yuv420p, ≤ 2:20 on
non-Premium X (the upload traps: wait for the progress bar to finish before
posting, never re-attach the same file). A post with media is still a post:
the text has to work with the picture hidden.

## «What should I post?» — research first, then several posts

The user asks what to post, or names a topic in two words. The answer is
posts, not questions:

1. **Read the platform first.** X: the trends / Explore page for the catalog of
   what is moving, then the top posts of the trend that fits the account; a
   search for the topic for how it is argued right now; the replies under the
   posts that lead for the objections. Reddit: the sub sorted by top and a
   Reddit search — Reddit material comes from Reddit, never from X. Threads:
   a Threads search. News and what people search: Google News, Google
   autocomplete. All of it in the browser, or with whatever search or
   social-data tool is connected.
2. **Scouted posts are raw material.** Take a hook, an angle, a format, an
   insight and write it in this account's voice about this account's thing;
   never copy a post, never keep its links, handles or @mentions, never quote
   it without a quote post (the `social-quote` skill). Each draft maps loosely
   to a different source post or a different angle on the theme.
3. **Write several finished posts** in visibly different forms (hot take,
   question, observation, one-liner, mini-list, story) and let the user point.
   Default three to five; a «batch of ten» is ten distinct angles, not ten
   rewrites. Each post is shown with its angle in one line and, when it riffs
   on a scouted post, the reference to it (so the user can see the source and
   the picture can be inherited when it is theirs to use).
4. **Promo is the minority.** At most one post in five carries the account's
   product; on Reddit none. The rest is what people on the topic actually want
   to read.
5. Say honestly when a read returned nothing; do not pad the batch with
   platitudes.

## Scheduling and rhythm

- A post is scheduled inside the posting hours in the user's timezone; it
  waits in the working folder until then.
- Spacing: X two hours between posts, one thread a day, a quote or a reply does
  not count; Threads similar; Reddit one submission per sub per day at most and
  not the same post in two subs the same hour.
- A rubric on a schedule gives the slots; the slots are the rhythm, the text is
  yours.

## Handing over

Write the post into a dated `.md` in the working folder (for Reddit: the
title, the body and the subreddit at the top, as `https://www.reddit.com/r/<sub>/`),
with the angle in one line and the media files next to it. Show it to the
user; nothing is posted until they say so. Once they say «post it», the text is
frozen — to change it, re-draft, do not edit in the composer. After it is live,
note the URL in the file. When the user wrote the post themselves and wants it
kept, save it the same way, with its URL.

## After it is live

- Note under `post:learned` in `NOTES.md`: which hook opened, which form got
  replies, which topic to drop, what the user changed in review (the review
  edits are the most valuable signal there is: a post they rewrote tells you
  the voice better than ten they approved).
- Replies under the post are the `social-reply` skill's business (watching the
  user's own posts); a post that did well is a thread or an article next week.
- The twenty-first post should need almost no briefing if the first twenty
  were written down.

## Checklist before the user sees it

- first line is the hook; one idea; a concrete thing in it; a point of view
- in the audience's language, in the account's register, nothing from the voice rules
- inside the platform's limit (280 non-Premium X; 500 Threads; Reddit title
  ≤ 300, plain, no self-promo, ends on a question; sub rules read)
- the user's link verbatim and where it belongs; no invented sources or numbers
- media files next to the text, four max, video H.264; the text works without the picture
- not a repeat of the last twenty; not the same thing as the queued ones
- promo at most one in five, none on Reddit
- scheduled inside posting hours; the angle stated in one line in the note
